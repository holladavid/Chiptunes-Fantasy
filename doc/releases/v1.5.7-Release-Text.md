# GitHub Release Description

**Von Nerds für Nerds:** v1.5.7 gives Chiptunes Fantasy the oscilloscope its music deserves. Instead of stretching one scalar value per display frame into a waveform, the new instrument reads thousands of real PCM samples, locks onto the signal, and draws a fast left-to-right CRT sweep with a short phosphor memory. A redesigned logarithmic FFT analyzer completes the hardware-lab front panel.

This release also refines the Scene DJ's musical pacing and several existing DSEs, fixes responsive Starfield distribution plus C64/Paula rendering edge cases, establishes written DSE development rules, and introduces the custom CF pixel-chip icon for browser, iOS, and PWA installations.

The curated collection remains **213 directly playable tracks: 73 Amiga, 54 C64, and 86 Atari ST**. Music is still parsed and synthesized in real time—no MP3s or prerecorded audio, and no replacement of the existing SID, Paula, or YM engines.

## Highlights

- **Real audio-rate waveform:** 4096-sample analyser capture and a sample-rate-derived **32 ms** window replace the old 60 Hz scalar history.
- **Stable triggered display:** DC removal, interpolated rising zero-crossing trigger, per-column min/max rasterization, and 160 amplitude levels keep chip waveforms readable and impulses visible.
- **CRT sweep with separate persistence:** a bright **32 ms** beam and a dim **420 ms** reference trace remain legible without drawing a false retrace line or accumulating an opaque waveform carpet.
- **Segmented logarithmic FFT:** 48 bars from **45 Hz to 12 kHz**, system palettes, LED matrix, frequency ruler, cached grid, reused arrays, and time-based peak hold/gravity.
- **More deliberate musical pacing:** slower tension growth in moderate passages, full-board acceleration when the music earns it, and distinct playing/buildup phases.
- **Refined universal DSEs:** beat-switched Starfield warp/vortex patterns, Copperbar formations, and bitmap TrackPresenter layouts; widescreen and rotation continuity included.
- **New CF identity:** SVG favicon, opaque Apple touch icon, and regular/maskable PWA icons.
- **Documented DSE craft:** explicit rules for music response, period pixels, responsive behavior, determinism, performance, and acceptance.

## New: a real triggered PCM oscilloscope

The oscilloscope now reads `getFloatTimeDomainData()` from the existing master-path `AnalyserNode`, with a byte-domain fallback. Its reused 4096-sample buffer provides real audio time-domain information; the canvas refresh rate no longer limits the number of samples represented by a trace.

Each analysis window covers **32 ms**, calculated from the actual `AudioContext.sampleRate`:

| Audio sample rate | Samples in one 32 ms window |
|---|---:|
| 48 kHz | 1,536 |
| 44.1 kHz | 1,411 |

The complete analyser buffer supplies the DC estimate. A hysteresis-armed rising zero-crossing trigger then finds a stable starting point and linearly interpolates the crossing. For display, every pixel column receives the minimum and maximum of all samples assigned to it, so even a single-sample impulse remains visible instead of disappearing between point samples.

### Phosphor behavior without visual mud

A fast beam draws each trace from left to right in **32 ms**, adding only newly reached columns. Completed traces move to a separate, dimmed memory canvas and fade over **420 ms**. The bright current trace and older reference therefore remain visually distinct, and the beam's return to the left stays blank.

The retro treatment uses fixed amplitude gain and up to **160 quantized vertical levels**. Rendering work is bounded through reused PCM/raster arrays, a reduced-resolution internal canvas, and a fixed pool of **64 sparks**. A wide transparent outer stroke supplies waveform glow; expensive soft shadow is limited to the small beam head.

Resize preserves both trace surfaces and the PCM/raster coherence of an active sweep. Pause and clear paths use elapsed-time/state resets rather than relying on a particular display refresh rate.

## Redesigned FFT phosphor analyzer

The spectrum display now presents **48 logarithmic bars from 45 Hz to 12 kHz**, backed by the existing **4096-point FFT / 2,048 analyser bins**. Bin average and peak information are combined with a moderate high-frequency lift, preserving musical contrast instead of making every bar look uniformly loud.

Each system receives its own palette. Segmented LED bars sit over an unlit matrix, measurement guides, and a logarithmic frequency ruler, creating an instrument panel that belongs beside the new scope.

Analyser sampling is capped at **30 Hz**. Typed arrays, bin ranges, segment colors, and the static grid are reused or cached, while attack/release, **0.42-second peak hold**, and peak gravity use elapsed time. The result remains consistent at 30, 60, 120 Hz, and other display refresh rates.

## Scene DJ and DSE refinements

### Tension with room to breathe

Moderate passages accumulate tension more slowly, while strong activity across every physical channel, loudness, and active beats can accelerate the build. A **55% buildup threshold** and intensity-dependent hold keep **playing** and **buildup** distinguishable before climax. Hysteresis still prevents state flutter.

On C64, the fourth telemetry slot is correctly excluded from analogue channel energy: it contains SID write rate, not a fourth audio voice. Exact beat events are routed separately from the decaying beat envelope so pattern changes happen on musical events rather than on every rendered frame.

| Effect | v1.5.7 refinement |
|---|---|
| **Starfield** | Three exact-beat-switched warp/vortex patterns, deterministic recycling, aliased trails, and system-specific motion. Widescreen coverage and immediate redistribution after mobile rotation remove clustered stars and large empty regions without restarting the effect. |
| **Copperbars** | Three fourth-beat-switched formations, alternating beat direction, buildup/climax punch and twist, and a dedicated afterglow treatment. |
| **TrackPresenter** | Aliased 5×7 bitmap typography, fourth-beat layout changes, state-specific colors/motion, and deterministic rotation-safe composition. |

The universal `void` effect remains outside this refinement pass.

### C64 sunset and Paula silicon-floor repairs

The C64 sunset now derives the sun's horizon offset from its rasterized radius. Narrow portrait buffers therefore retain the intended visible sun instead of pushing all of its rows below the horizon.

Paula silicon-floor cache hits now receive instance-owned canvas copies. A later resize can no longer deform shared cached moiré/grid snapshots or another viewport's raster surfaces.

### Written DSE development rules

`doc/concepts/dse_development_rules.md` now defines the implementation and acceptance contract for future effects: exact beats versus envelopes, macro-state behavior, channel ownership, historical palettes and internal resolutions, integer rasterization, ordered-dither fades, responsive world projection, same-instance rotation, deterministic resources, caching, bounded degradation, and visual/production-browser evidence.

## Suggested listening and visual checks

1. Play **Commando** on C64, **Space Debris** on Amiga, and **Goldrunner** on Atari. Compare the triggered waveform shapes and the system-specific FFT palettes.
2. Watch the beam during quiet and dense passages: the current trace should remain bright, the previous trace dim, and retrace should not create a horizontal line.
3. Resize the window or rotate a phone during Starfield. Stars should redistribute across the new viewport while the same effect continues.
4. Compare moderate and full-board passages to hear/see the slower playing → buildup → climax pacing.
5. Inspect Copperbars and TrackPresenter around fourth-beat boundaries and during afterglow.
6. Install or reopen the PWA on the intended physical iPhone and verify the home-screen icon, safe areas, fullscreen variant, and touch behavior.

## Scope and known limitations

- No SID, Paula, or YM synthesis-engine replacement was required; the release changes visualization, DSE behavior, app artwork, and documentation.
- The scope is a triggered musical instrument visualization, not a calibrated laboratory measurement device.
- Historical constraints apply to individual DSEs. The Scene DJ's arbitrary multi-layer compositions remain intentionally fantastical rather than claims of single-machine feasibility.
- Headless/mobile-viewport checks are not physical-iPhone certification. Native iOS fullscreen and PWA behavior can vary by browser, iOS version, and installation mode.
- Render timings are not frame-rate or audio-underrun guarantees for other devices.
- The known pre-existing **SID synthetic-corpus mismatch** remains outside this release's scope; no failing baseline was overwritten. C64/SID random-access seeking remains unavailable.
- No tracks or playlist paths were added or removed, and no new music-redistribution or licensing claim is made.

## Upgrade and run locally

Use the complete **v1.5.7 source tree**, including the visualization modules, DSE files, manifest, and new icon assets. No dependency update, data migration, or audio-cache rebuild is required. If an installed PWA retains stale assets or its old icon, reload after its offline cache is updated or cleared; home-screen icon refresh behavior can be OS-controlled.

Serve the source tree over HTTP(S); ES modules and `AudioWorklet` loading are not intended for `file://` URLs.

```bash
npx serve .
# or
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Open the shown local URL, enter through the boot screen, and select **AMIGA**, **COMMODORE 64**, or **ATARI ST**.
