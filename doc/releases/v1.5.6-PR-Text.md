# Pull Request Title

**feat: release v1.5.6 with Scene DJ, mobile, and Assembly C64 upgrades**

# Pull Request Description

## Summary

This PR prepares Chiptunes Fantasy **v1.5.6** as a complete visualization, mobile, and music-curation release. It combines the original iPhone/fullscreen repairs, eight Scene-DJ/DSE development phases, window-mode and mobile-reading improvements, and four verified Assembly C64 overall-winner title tracks with museum entries.

The curated library grows from **209 to 213 directly playable tracks**: **73 Amiga**, **54 C64**, and **86 Atari ST** titles. Existing playlist paths are preserved. No complete-computer visualization emulator or replacement audio engine is introduced.

## Changes

### 1. Scene DJ and DSE optimization — phases 1–8

#### Phase 1: reproducible baseline and rotation

- Adds a repeatable browser capture harness for **20 managed DSE/system combinations**.
- Captures portrait and landscape stills, motion sequences, determinism/beat diagnostics, and live portrait → landscape → portrait rotation.
- Verifies that rotation retains the same DSE instance instead of restarting the effect.

#### Phase 2: register-driven music response and scheduling

- Forwards live SID, Paula, and YM register snapshots through the Scene DJ to the Track Monitor.
- Treats the first register snapshot as a baseline, eliminating startup phantom beats.
- Uses a shared cooldown for hardware and analogue beat paths and resets register, energy, and beat history on track/system changes.
- Stabilizes buildup/playing transitions with hysteresis.
- Holds managed DSE swaps until an accepted beat, retains immediate afterglow handling, and provides a bounded two-second beatless fallback.
- Prevents duplicate registration of the same DSE instance.

#### Phase 3: historical visual constraints

- Introduces C64 palette mapping, Amiga 12-bit quantization, and Atari 9-bit quantization.
- Uses low internal resolutions and nearest-neighbor presentation rather than browser smoothing.
- Replaces anti-aliased canvas edges with integer/aliased circle, line, and polygon rasterizers where required.
- Applies the historical target to each DSE individually; arbitrary multi-layer mixes remain explicitly fantastical.

#### Phase 4: pixel-identical render optimization

- Optimizes repeated drawing work with span-based rasterization and caching strategies.
- Preserves the reviewed output pixels rather than trading visual identity for speed.

#### Phase 5: palette-safe transitions

- Replaces alpha-blended DSE fades with ordered-dither visibility patterns.
- Keeps transitions inside the active C64, Amiga, or Atari palette instead of generating browser-intermediate colors.

#### Phase 6: PAL-style 50 Hz rendering and layer caches

- Caps DSE image calculation at **50 Hz** and stores transparent per-layer frame caches.
- Invalidates caches on PAL tick, DSE state, system, or size changes.
- Keeps DJ logic, beat analysis, and state transitions at full time resolution.
- Preserves z-order and the Scene DJ's existing layer-composition model.

#### Phase 7: visible hardware-like beat reactions

- Adds observable beat responses to previously passive silicon, copperbar, and related effects.
- Extends regression coverage across stills, motion, palettes, transitions, rotation, determinism, and real production-browser behavior.

#### Phase 8: cold-start and long-running stability

- Caches expensive Paula raster structures to reduce cold-start work.
- Re-runs identity comparisons after optimization and adds mixed-layer stress coverage with repeated orientation changes.
- Retains beat response, deterministic diagnostics, and DSE instance continuity.

## Scope and boundaries

- The historical-feasibility goal applies to **individual DSEs**, not to the Scene DJ's arbitrary mix of multiple layers. This is a period-oriented visualization model, not a full C64/Amiga/Atari emulator.
- No SID, Paula, or YM synthesis-engine replacement is included. The existing audio-worklet architectures and playback formats are retained.
- DSE render-time and palette checks are regression evidence, not cycle-exact hardware-feasibility proof.
- The phase-7 motion audit reports **two palette-violation samples**. Phase 8 preserves that reviewed corpus identically; it does not claim those samples were repaired.
- Offline audio/render timings do not guarantee physical-device underrun freedom or frame rates.
- Headless Chromium and simulated safe areas do not replace final physical-iPhone/WebKit acceptance.
- The known pre-existing **SID synthetic-corpus Phase 3 hash mismatch** remains disclosed; no failing baseline was overwritten. C64/SID random-access seeking remains unavailable.
- Public archive/HVSC availability does not grant public-domain, redistribution, or commercial-use rights. Rights remain with their respective holders.
