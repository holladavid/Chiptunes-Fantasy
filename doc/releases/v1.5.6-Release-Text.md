# GitHub Release Description

**Von Nerds für Nerds:** v1.5.6 is a substantial visualization, mobile, and curation release. Eight development phases make the Scene DJ more music-aware and its individual Demo-Scene-Elements more period-oriented, while new window/fullscreen behavior keeps the stage unobstructed and mobile reading controls within reach.

The release also adds four verified Assembly C64 overall-winner title tracks with museum research. The curated collection now contains **213 directly playable tracks**: **73 Amiga**, **54 C64**, and **86 Atari ST** titles. Every previous playlist path remains available.

No MP3s or prerecorded audio are used. Historical binary music formats are parsed and synthesized in real time; the new Assembly entries use the existing SID AudioWorklet path.

## Highlights

- **Eight DSE phases:** reproducible visual baselines, register-driven beat detection, activity scheduling, historical palettes and rasterization, pixel-identical optimization, ordered-dither transitions, PAL-style 50 Hz rendering, visible beat reactions, and cold-start/stability work.
- **Historically constrained individual effects:** low internal resolutions, nearest-neighbor scaling, palette-safe colors, and aliased integer drawing replace browser anti-aliasing where required. The Scene DJ's layer mix remains intentionally fantastical.
- **Truly unobstructed playback:** transient overlays auto-hide in windowed mode and fullscreen, including the expanded Lab HUD and fullscreen **EXIT** control; interaction wakes them again.
- **Better mobile reading and listening:** compact transport/system controls, iPhone safe-area handling, and a sticky Previous/Play/Next bar over Living Silicon and museum articles. Next Track preserves the current scroll position.
- **Four Assembly C64 winners:** title tracks from 2012, 2020, 2023, and 2024, selected from a documented 2006–2025 review and accompanied by museum entries, provenance, and source links.
- **54-track C64 acceptance:** every C64 entry passes audible, finite, deterministic, block-size-independent, and restart-identical offline checks.

## Scene DJ and DSE development — phases 1–8

| Phase | Delivered improvement |
|---|---|
| **1 — Baseline and rotation** | Established a repeatable capture harness for 20 managed DSE/system combinations in portrait and landscape. Live portrait → landscape → portrait rotation preserves the same DSE instance. |
| **2 — Music and activity** | Forwarded real chip registers into visual analysis, primed the first snapshot without a phantom beat, unified beat cooldowns, reset stale state on track/system changes, stabilized buildup transitions, and scheduled DSE swaps on accepted beats with a bounded beatless fallback. |
| **3 — Historical pixels and palettes** | Added C64 palette mapping, Amiga 12-bit and Atari 9-bit quantization, low internal resolutions, nearest-neighbor scaling, and aliased circle/line/polygon rasterizers. |
| **4 — Pixel-identical optimization** | Reduced rendering overhead with span-based and cached drawing strategies while preserving the reviewed output pixels. |
| **5 — Palette-safe transitions** | Replaced alpha-generated intermediate colors with ordered-dither fades constrained to the active system palette. |
| **6 — PAL timing and layer caches** | Limited DSE image calculation to PAL-style 50 Hz and cached transparent layers. DJ logic, beat analysis, and state changes keep their full time resolution. |
| **7 — Visible beat response** | Gave previously passive silicon, copperbar, and other effects observable hardware-like beat reactions, backed by motion, palette, rotation, determinism, and production-browser regression checks. |
| **8 — Cold start and stability** | Reduced Paula cold-start raster work and verified pixel identity, diagnostics, rotation, and mixed-layer stress behavior after optimization. |

The historical target applies to **each DSE on its own**, not to the feasibility of an arbitrary multi-layer mix on one historical computer. This release does not add a complete C64, Amiga, or Atari emulator for visualization.

## Window mode, fullscreen, and mobile

### Automatic overlay hiding

The Now Playing / **BUS ACTIVE** card, stage controls, logo, and hardware overlays now follow inactivity hiding in windowed mode as well as fullscreen. The expanded Lab HUD is included. Touch, pointer, or stage interaction restores the interface.

Fullscreen no longer keeps **EXIT** permanently visible. After inactivity, the complete fullscreen interface disappears; interact with the stage to reveal the controls and leave fullscreen as before.

### Safe areas and compact controls

The stage accounts for device cutouts so the Chiptunes Fantasy logo and **Lab Mode** remain reachable below an iPhone notch. Mobile transport uses one row, progress and volume share another, and computer/core selection uses tighter spacing. The oversized mobile fullscreen panel no longer consumes the stage.

At the tested **393 × 659 px** viewport, the playback bar measures **95 px instead of 255 px**, returning space to visualization and song selection without removing controls.

### Sticky transport while reading

On mobile, Previous/Play/Next stays at the top of the viewport while scrolling through Living Silicon or museum articles. **Next Track** advances playback without resetting the reading position, and the bar remains usable after rotation to landscape.

## Assembly C64 winners — 2006–2025 review

Only independently verified C64 **overall winners** of the Assembly Oldskool/Mixed Demo competition were included. Non-C64 winners and lower-placed C64 entries were not used as substitutes.

| Year | Winning production | Title track | Supplied music source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | *Conservative Megademo* | *Playhorse* — Paavo Pessi (Ilmarque) | Unmodified HVSC #85 bytes |
| 2020 | *Fantomas* | *Nine Nights from the Black Moon* — Jani Joeli (Jojeli) | Unmodified HVSC #85 bytes |
| 2023 | *The Scroll of Antonius* | *The Scroll of Antonius* — Figge Wasberger (Fegolhuzz) | Unmodified HVSC #85 bytes |
| 2024 | *Transcend the Game* | *Transcend the Game* — Ville Heikkilä (Viznut) | New PSID-v2 rip from the original PRG |

The 2024 rip preserves the complete `$0400–$0AFF` player/music region, including pattern and instrument data referenced through `$09xx` and `$0Axx`. Independent 6502 analysis exercised **15,000 play calls**, producing **224,443 SID register writes**; musical register activity occurred in 14,993 frames. No exact full-state loop is asserted.

The 53 HVSC-backed C64 files use matched song-length lists. The 2024 rip has no official length in the supplied provenance and therefore uses the existing **180-second fallback**. Each Assembly track has its own museum entry, source links, and hash-bound provenance.

## Suggested listening and device checks

1. Start a track on each system and watch how register activity, beats, energy, and DSE changes interact; rotate a phone during an active effect and confirm visual continuity.
2. Leave the stage untouched in windowed mode and fullscreen: transient overlays should disappear, then return on interaction. In fullscreen, **EXIT** should hide with the rest of the interface.
3. Scroll into a museum article or Living Silicon on mobile and use **Next Track**; playback should change while the article remains at the same scroll position.
4. Open **COMMODORE 64 → assembly** and compare the four title tracks and their museum cards.
5. On a physical notched iPhone, verify safe-area placement, browser/PWA fullscreen behavior, and touch wake-up in the intended Safari mode.

## Scope and known limitations

- No SID, Paula, or YM synthesis engine replacement was required. The release changes visualization/analysis, responsive presentation, curated content, and their verification—not the fundamental audio-worklet architectures.
- DSE checks demonstrate internal consistency, palette constraints, and regression behavior; they do not prove cycle-exact historical feasibility. The phase-7 motion audit still reports **two palette-violation samples**. Phase 8 is pixel-identical to that reviewed corpus and does not claim their removal.
- Headless Chromium viewports and simulated safe areas are not physical-iPhone certification. Native iOS fullscreen behavior can vary by browser, iOS version, and PWA mode.
- Offline timing ratios and render measurements are not device-specific audio-underrun or frame-rate guarantees.
- The 2024 Assembly track uses a 180-second duration fallback, not an official song length or a proven exact loop.
- The known pre-existing **SID synthetic-corpus Phase 3 hash mismatch** remains disclosed, and no failing baseline was overwritten. C64/SID random-access seeking remains unavailable.
- Public availability in HVSC or a scene archive does not imply public domain, redistribution permission, or commercial-use rights. Rights remain with their respective holders.

## Upgrade and run locally

Use the complete v1.5.6 source tree rather than replacing only the stylesheet: this release includes visualization modules, DSEs, tests, playlist/museum data, and new SID files. No dependency update, data migration, or audio-cache rebuild is required. If an installed PWA retains stale assets, reload after its offline cache is updated or cleared.

Serve the source tree over HTTP(S); ES modules and `AudioWorklet` loading are not intended for `file://` URLs.

```bash
npx serve .
# or
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Open the shown local URL, enter through the boot screen, and select **AMIGA**, **COMMODORE 64**, or **ATARI ST**.
